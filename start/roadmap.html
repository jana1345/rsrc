The 16-week roadmap ​

This schedule keeps the three-month structure from GNC.md and adds the Month 4 flight-software track. It assumes 12–15 focused hours per week. At 8–10 hours, stretch it to 24–30 weeks. Do not compress Month 2 to catch up; the GNC core is the part that compounds.

The weeks are a route, not a contract. If a topic takes two weeks, take two weeks. If you already have the skill, prove it with the deliverable and move on.

At a glance ​

MonthWeeksFocusExit deliverable
1 · Foundations1–4Space basics, frames primer, STK, MATLAB/Simulink, C++, Linux, Git, CMake, debuggingA small C++ lander sim, a lunar STK scenario, and a 1D MATLAB/Simulink model
2 · GNC core5–10Frames/time, quaternions, attitude dynamics, PID/LQR, 6DOF, Monte Carlo, navigation, guidance, architectureA closed-loop 6DOF descent sim with EKF, controller, guidance, and dispersion results
3 · Test & integration11–12V&V, SIL/HIL, Python automation, STK automationA test plan, automated test suite, and a repeatable STK study
4 · Flight software13–16Real-time C++, Simulink pipeline, cFS, RTOS, CCSDS, NPR 7150.2, fault protection, sensorsA small flight-software app with requirements, tests, timing evidence, and a fault/mode design

Month 1 · Foundations (weeks 1–4) ​

WeekTopicsBuild
1Space basics, frames primer, lunar environmentWrite a one-page mission profile: PDI altitude, descent phases, key frames, and the numbers you will use.
2STK, MATLAB & SimulinkBuild a lunar orbit/landing-site scenario in STK. Build a 1D vertical lander in Simulink.
3Modern C++, LinuxWrite a fixed-memory quaternion and state type in C++ and run it on the command line.
4Git, CMake, debuggingPut the C++ sim in a repo with CMake, tests, a debug build, and a sanitizer run.

Month 1 done when: you can run a 1D lander sim, explain every frame and unit in it, reproduce a bug with a debugger, and show a clean build from a fresh clone.

Month 2 · GNC core (weeks 5–10) ​

This is the heart of the plan. Do not rush it.

WeekTopicsBuild
5Frames, time & transformations, quaternionsImplement MCMF → ENU → body transforms and a quaternion attitude state. Test with known vectors.
6Attitude dynamicsAdd rigid-body rotational dynamics, inertia, RCS torque, and a thrust vector to the sim.
7PID & LQRClose an attitude loop. Compare PID and LQR. Add saturation, rate limits, and a deadband.
86DOF simulation, Monte CarloBuild the full plant. Add sensors, actuators, mass depletion, and a fixed-step integrator. Run dispersions.
9Navigation, EKF & sensor fusionAdd a noisy IMU and altimeter. Estimate altitude, vertical speed, attitude, and gyro bias. Check consistency.
10Powered descent guidance, GNC architectureGenerate throttle/attitude commands from the estimated state. Draw the architecture and rate groups.

Month 2 done when: the vehicle can descend from PDI to a target under sensor noise, the controller stays stable at the flight rate, the EKF is consistent, and a 100-run Monte Carlo produces a landing dispersion you can explain.

The capstone ​

Build one artifact you can show: a lunar powered-descent flight-software demo with evidence.

Minimum scope:

  1. Plant: 6DOF lunar descent from PDI, with gravity, thrust, mass flow, RCS, and a landing target.
  2. Navigation: IMU + altimeter EKF (attitude may be truth or star-tracker-aided) with noisy measurements.
  3. Guidance: phase logic and a throttle/attitude command that reaches the target.
  4. Control: PID or LQR attitude loop at a realistic rate, with saturation and deadbands.
  5. Flight software: fixed-memory C++ app, message interfaces, mode manager, watchdog, and fault handling.
  6. V&V: requirements, traceability, unit tests, SIL run, fault injection, and a Monte Carlo report.
  7. Story: a five-minute explanation of the architecture, the three biggest risks, and the evidence that retires them.

If you can defend that, you can hold a conversation with a lunar-lander flight-software team.

Where this deviates from GNC.md ​

Original planThis roadmapWhy
STK in Month 1 before coordinate framesShort frames primer before STK; full treatment in Month 2STK is an exercise in frames and time. Without them, you learn buttons, not astrodynamics.
Month 1 has eight broad topicsCompressed tooling, more time on C++/Linux/debugging and the mini simTool fluency is necessary but not the differentiator. The build is.
Month 2 has six topicsAdded navigation, guidance, and architectureThese are the must-master items from GNC.md; they cannot be left implicit.
Month 3 is SIL/HIL, Python, STK automationAdded V&V and made it continuousTest evidence is a hard constraint for one-shot landing roles.
No cFS/RTOS/CCSDS/NPRMonth 4 flight-software trackThese appear in the skills table and in job descriptions.
“Orbital mechanics lower priority”Scoped mission page: two-body, frames, time, descentYou do not need the full three-body problem, but you do need the descent-relevant parts.

If you only have six weeks ​

Do this, in order:

  1. Frames/time + quaternions.
  2. 6DOF sim + attitude control.
  3. Navigation/EKF basics.
  4. Powered descent guidance.
  5. V&V + one automated test suite.
  6. Real-time C++ constraints + one cFS or RTOS exercise.

Skip STK automation, NPR 7150.2 depth, and the full Monte Carlo campaign. You will still be able to build and defend a descent demo.
